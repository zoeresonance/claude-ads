# Presence — Content & Audience Insights™
---

## Data & Methodology

We use a multifaceted approach to understand *Presence*'s readership and translate it into content that resonates. This knowledge forms the foundation of the brand's **Audience Segments** and "The One."

Our primary sources are the book itself — *Presence: Modern Birth, Primal Wisdom* by Margaret McKinney Buxton, DNP, CNM — and the internal strategy documents built around it: the **Audience Framework**, the **Content Matrix**, and the **Content Idea Bank**. Together these translate decades of midwifery practice and hundreds of birth stories into a repeatable messaging system.

We also draw on the brand's visual identity documentation (wordmark, palette, typography, layout principles) to ensure every piece of content stays visually and verbally consistent with the book's tone.

**Audience Segments** describe *who* the reader is — her generation, her life stage, and the worldview she reads the book through.

**Brand Pillars** describe *what* she needs to hear — the five universal emotional truths the book returns to again and again, regardless of segment.

Unlike a demographic data set built from external panels, this insight system is drawn directly from the author's clinical experience and the book's own language — it is qualitative and editorial by design, not projected or modeled.

## Section 1: Audience Segments — Generation & Life Experience

The audience for *Presence* isn't defined by a single demographic. Women connect with the book through three overlapping lenses: **Generation**, **Life Stage**, and **Worldview**. The most effective marketing begins with universal themes, then tailors language to each audience's unique points of resonance — the goal is never to change the message, but to illuminate the parts of it each reader is already primed to hear.

| Segment | Age Range | Core Needs | Messaging Style |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gen Z | 21–28 | Identity, authenticity, emotional honesty, autonomy, community | Direct, authentic, transparent, unpolished over polished |
| Millennial | 29–43 | Healing, intentional living, burnout recovery, becoming | Thoughtful, evidence-informed, emotionally intelligent |
| Gen X | 44–54 | Wisdom, legacy, healing old stories | Confident, respectful, understated |
| Grandmother / Mentor | 55+ | Passing on wisdom, supporting the next generation | Warm, affirming, restrained |

*Millennials are considered the book's primary audience.*

## Section 4: Universal Brand Pillars

These five themes transcend age, race, politics, and life stage. They anchor every major campaign and should lead broad content before segment-specific language narrows it.

| Pillar | Core Idea | Resonates With |
|---|---|---|
| **You Are Not Broken** | The emotional heart of the book. Speaks to women who have questioned their bodies, instincts, emotions, or worth — Presence begins with the assumption that women are already whole. | Every audience |
| **Trust Your Body / The Human Blueprint** | The body is not something to fight — it is something to understand. Presence invites women to reconnect with the wisdom and intuition that have always been within them. | Every expectant mother, regardless of birth philosophy |
| **We Were Never Meant To Do This Alone** | Community isn't a luxury — it's part of our design. Whether readers hear "family," "friends," "church," or "village," nearly everyone recognizes the loneliness of modern life. | Every generation, every worldview |
| **Birth Is Transformation** | Birth is more than an event — it is a profound process of becoming. The book shifts focus from the "perfect birth" to embracing one of life's greatest transformations with trust and support. | Every woman preparing to become a mother |
| **Presence Changes Everything** | The thread that ties the book together. When women are fully present — with themselves, their bodies, their babies, their communities — fear gives way to trust, isolation gives way to connection. | Every woman seeking a more connected, intentional life |

**Summary mapping:**
1. You Are Not Broken → Identity
2. Trust Your Body → Self
3. We Were Never Meant To Do This Alone → Community
4. Birth Is Transformation → The Journey
5. Presence Changes Everything → The Big Idea

## Section 9: The One — "Becoming Blair"

### Profile at a Glance

| Attribute | Detail |
|---|---|
| Name | Becoming Blair |
| Generation | Millennial (29–43) |
| Life Stage | Currently pregnant (first child) |
| Worldview Lean | Holds science and spirituality together; not strongly aligned with either political pole |
| Occupation | Works full-time, career-driven but reevaluating pace |
| Household | Partnered, planning to grow her family intentionally |

### Her Story

**Who She Is**

Blair is thoughtful, self-aware, and used to being competent at everything she tries. Pregnancy is the first major life event she can't fully research, plan, or optimize her way through, and that unsettles her more than she expected. She reads widely — parenting books, birth stories, wellness accounts — searching for a version of pregnancy that feels true rather than performative.

**Reconnecting With Her Body**

Blair grew up trusting external authorities — doctors, apps, algorithms — more than her own instincts. Pregnancy is beginning to reverse that. She's discovering that her body has been quietly wise all along, and she's hungry for language that validates what she's starting to feel rather than language that tells her what to fear next.

**Craving Community**

Blair has friends, but few of them are in this exact season of life with her. She feels the loneliness of modern motherhood before she's even given birth — the sense that generations before her had a village, and hers has a group chat. She wants real community, not just content.

**Facing the Culture Around Her**

Blair is aware of the "perfect pregnancy" performance — the curated bump photos, the birth plans presented as scripts, the pressure to have an opinion on everything from epidurals to elimination diets. She's tired of managing an image and wants permission to simply experience what's happening to her.

**Becoming, Not Performing**

Blair understands, intellectually, that birth is transformation — but she's only beginning to feel it emotionally. She's looking for a guide that treats this transformation with the weight it deserves, without adding another set of standards she has to meet.

**Her Relationship to Care**

Blair wants a provider relationship built on partnership, not compliance. She's drawn to language about informed consent, birth planning as communication rather than script, and providers who see her as a whole person moving through a major life passage — not a patient to be managed.

---

### Her Motivations

| Area | Motivation |
|---|---|
| Body | Reclaim trust in her own instincts after a lifetime of outsourcing authority to outside experts. |
| Community | Build a "village" she doesn't currently have — real people, not just content. |
| Identity | Move through the transformation of matrescence without losing herself or feeling broken by it. |
| Care | Find a provider and birth team who partner with her rather than manage her. |
| Culture | Reject the performance of the "perfect pregnancy" in favor of something honest. |
| Legacy | Begin building the kind of presence she hopes to one day pass on to her own child. |

---

### Messaging — Most Likely to Respond To

**Body & Trust**

**The Human Blueprint:** Affirm that her body carries innate wisdom built over generations — she is not starting from zero.

**Science + Instinct Together:** Hold evidence-informed information and intuitive trust as partners, never opposites.

**Permission Over Perfection:** Remind her there is no perfect pregnancy, birth, or parent — and that this is where real trust begins.

**Community**

**Village-Building:** Speak directly to the loneliness of modern motherhood and offer language for building real community.

**Shared Humanity:** Use collective language ("you are not on an island by yourself") that locates her experience inside a much larger human story.

**Transformation**

**Becoming, Not Just Birthing:** Frame birth as a passage that changes who she is, not just an event to get through.

**Matrescence:** Name the identity shift of motherhood directly — she is becoming someone new, and that deserves acknowledgment.

**Care & Agency**

**Empowered Birth:** Emphasize that she can know her birthing environment, choose and build relationships with her care providers, and come with intentions rather than a rigid script.

**Birth Planning as Communication:** Reframe the birth plan away from a fixed document and toward an ongoing conversation with her care team.

**Presence**

**Slowing Down:** Invite her to pause and give her full attention to what's actually happening, rather than rushing toward the next milestone.

**Presence as Practice:** Reinforce that presence isn't a destination — it's something she can practice, imperfectly, starting now.

---

### Messaging — Least Likely to Respond To

Blair disengages from anything that reintroduces the performance pressure she's trying to escape — messaging that implies she needs to do more, prepare harder, or "earn" a good birth outcome. Fear-based framing (worst-case scenarios, warning-heavy checklists, "what to watch for" language) reads as the same fatigue-inducing content she's already trying to move away from.

She's also unlikely to respond to clinical, detached language that treats her as a case to be managed rather than a person moving through transformation, or to messaging that assumes a single "correct" birth philosophy — natural, medicated, hospital, home — is morally superior to another. Content that positions any provider, place of birth, or feeding method as a referendum on her worth as a mother will alienate rather than reassure her.

Finally, avoid overloading her with more responsibility. She's already absorbing enormous physical, emotional, and identity change; messaging that adds pressure rather than presence works against the brand's core promise.
